What does a cost-benefit IAM do?

Cutting emissions costs money and emitting causes damage. A cost-benefit IAM looks for the level of emissions where the total of these two costs is as small as possible. That happens where the cost of cutting one more tonne equals the damage that tonne would cause, which is the crossing point on the graph. The price at that point is the social cost of carbon, and a carbon tax set at that level would deliver the optimal emissions. Models such as DICE do exactly this, only over many years and with a climate model linking emissions to temperature and damages.

Exercise

The world emits 50 GtCO₂ per year if nothing is done. Find the emissions level and carbon price a cost-benefit IAM would recommend.

Let E be global emissions in GtCO₂ per year. With no action, E = 50. When emissions are E, the cost of cutting one more tonne is 4(50 − E) dollars, and the damage from one more tonne is E dollars.

1. Find the emissions level where the two are equal. What are the optimal emissions and carbon price?

2. Suppose damages turn out to be twice as large, so the damage from one more tonne is 2E dollars. Find the new optimal emissions and carbon price. Did the carbon price double? Explain why or why not.
