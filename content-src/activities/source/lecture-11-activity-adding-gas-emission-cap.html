Adding Gas and an Emission Cap: Solving a Power System by Hand

Work in pairs. Time: about 30 minutes. Only a calculator is needed. Round final answers to two decimal places and show the working in the boxes.

The system

Westeros must supply 50 GWa of electricity. It has three power plants. Gas is new: it costs more to run than coal but emits less than half as much CO₂ per unit of electricity. The data are illustrative teaching values.

Coal60458.0
Gas80503.4
Wind20300

Useful facts. Output (GWa) × running cost (USD/kWa) = cost in million USD. Output (GWa) × emission factor (t CO₂/kWa) = emissions in Mt CO₂. Write xc, xg and xw for the output of coal, gas and wind in GWa.

Part A. No climate policy

A1. Rank the three plants from cheapest to most expensive to run. This ranking is called the merit order.

A3. Why is gas not used at all in this solution?

Part B. Adding an emission cap of 100 Mt CO₂

The government now limits total emissions:

8.0 xc + 3.4 xg + 0 xw ≤ 100  (Mt CO₂)

B1. Does the answer to A2 satisfy the cap? By how much does it exceed it?

B2. Should wind still run at its maximum of 30 GWa? Explain in one sentence.

B3. With wind at 30 GWa, how much demand is left for coal and gas together? Write this as an equation in xc and xg.

B4. Try two extreme options and fill in the table. Which one meets the cap?

B5. Coal is cheaper than gas, so the cheapest solution uses as much coal as the cap allows. Use the equation from B3 and the cap (with equality) to find xc and xg.

B6. Calculate the total cost of this solution, including wind.

B7. How much did the cap raise the total cost compared with A2?

Part C. The carbon price by hand

The carbon price is the cost of reducing emissions by one more tonne, given that the cheapest options are already in use. In this system the only way to cut emissions further is to switch more output from coal to gas.

C1. Switching 1 kWa from coal to gas: how much extra does it cost (USD), and how many tonnes of CO₂ does it avoid?

C2. What is the cost per tonne of CO₂ avoided? This is the carbon price.

C3. Check the answer: tighten the cap to 99 Mt and repeat B5 and B6. By how much does the total cost rise?

Part D. The price of electricity

D1. With the 100 Mt cap still in place, demand rises to 51 GWa. Wind is already at its maximum. Find the new xc, xg and total cost.

D2. By how much did the cost rise for one extra GWa? This is the price of electricity in USD/kWa.

D3. Show that this price equals the running cost of gas plus the carbon price times the emissions of gas.

D4. Without the cap the electricity price was 60 USD/kWa (the cost of coal). Explain in one sentence why the cap raised it.

Part E. What if? (choose one, then discuss)

E1. A carbon tax instead of a cap. Replace the cap with a tax of 10 USD per tonne. Add the tax to each plant’s running cost (tax × emission factor), find the new merit order and the cheapest dispatch. What are the emissions?

E2. A tax equal to the carbon price. Repeat E1 with a tax of 4.35 USD per tonne. What do you notice about coal and gas?

E3. A new zero-carbon option. Solar becomes available at 15 USD/kWa, up to 10 GWa, with zero emissions (the 100 Mt cap is restored). Find the cheapest dispatch, its emissions, and the carbon price.
