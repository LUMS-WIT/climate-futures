# Module 2: Scenarios & Integrated Assessment

**Weeks 4–6**

This module covers the scenario-based tools used by researchers and governments to evaluate policy before implementation, including their methodological limitations.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the function of an SSP narrative and the rationale for using scenario families rather than single forecasts.
- Use an integrated assessment model to trace the outcome of a mitigation pathway.
- Connect carbon-pricing and fiscal mechanisms to national policy case studies.

## Guiding Question

A model's output is only as reliable as its underlying assumptions — which assumptions in these models warrant the greatest scrutiny?

## Lectures

(lec6)=
### Lecture 6: Introduction to IPCC and Scenarios

An overview of the IPCC and the international climate negotiation process, the SSP and RCP scenario families used to project the future, and how integrated assessment models link climate, energy, water, and land to connect global scenarios with Pakistan-specific policy frameworks such as NDCs and the Enhanced Transparency Framework.

(lec7)=
### Lecture 7: Representative Concentration Pathways (RCPs)

The history of IPCC emissions scenarios and the parallel process behind the newer scenario framework, the definition of a scenario as a conditional description of a possible future rather than a forecast, and the four Representative Concentration Pathways (RCP2.6, RCP4.5, RCP6.0, RCP8.5) with the integrated assessment models used to develop them. Includes an in-class activity using the IIASA RCP Database.

(lec8)=
### Lecture 8: Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs)

The five Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSP1 Sustainability, SSP2 Middle of the Road, SSP3 Regional Rivalry, SSP4 Inequality, SSP5 Fossil-fueled Development), their qualitative narratives and quantitative drivers such as population, GDP, and urbanization, and the reference scenarios produced by six integrated assessment modelling teams. The lecture then covers the SSP-RCP scenario matrix used in CMIP6 and closes with guidance on how a planner should select a scenario for long-lived infrastructure. Includes an in-class activity in which students match short narratives to SSP-RCP combinations.
